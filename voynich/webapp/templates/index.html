Run a decipherment attempt

Trains on even-numbered lines, validates on held-out odd lines. The verdict compares the held-out score against a random-key floor and a real-language ceiling. A sweep runs the current configuration against every reference language and ranks the results — the chosen reference above is ignored.

Validate the solver on a cipher with a known answer

Encrypts a held-out slice of the reference corpus with a random substitution and checks whether the annealer recovers the plaintext. This is the proof that the machinery can solve what is solvable.

Self-citation diagnostics (is it generated text?)

The leading non-language theory (Timm & Schinner) holds that each Voynich word was produced by copying and mutating a recently written word. This test measures that fingerprint directly: how often does a word have a near-duplicate among the previous N words, compared with the same text shuffled, and compared with real languages? No key search involved — this interrogates the text itself.

Datasets

    Voynich text: word-level interlinear transcription (Takahashi line), via the chirila/Voynich-public corpus. References: De Imitatione Christi + De Bello Gallico (Latin), La Divina Commedia (Italian), King James Bible (English).

    What this tool can and cannot claim

    The Voynich Manuscript (Beinecke MS 408) is undeciphered. No software can promise to solve it; anyone claiming otherwise is selling something. What this workbench does is make decipherment hypotheses testable and falsifiable:

    • A hypothesis is a family of substitution keys (simple or position-dependent) from EVA glyph tokens to letters of a target language.
    • Candidate keys are scored by how probable the decoded text is under a character n-gram model of a real corpus — medieval Latin contemporary with the manuscript's 1404–1438 carbon dating, 14th-century Italian, or early-modern English.
    • Simulated annealing searches the key space; held-out validation on alternating lines guards against the optimizer simply memorising the training text.
    • The benchmark tab proves the search machinery recovers genuine substitution ciphers (typically 100% of letters within seconds) — so a failure on Voynichese is evidence about the text, not about the solver.

    The consistent scientific finding — reproduced by this tool — is that no simple or positional substitution into Latin, Italian or English makes Voynichese read as language, even though its rigid word structure lets optimizers close much of the statistical gap. Negative results here are real results.
